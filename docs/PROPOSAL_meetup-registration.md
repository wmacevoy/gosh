## 1. What is wrong today

A meetup has one key, shared by everyone allowed to use it. To have a datagram passed on, an
end puts twelve bytes in front of it: four random bytes and a tag of those four bytes. They
travel in the clear, say nothing of who sent them, when, or for which pair, and the meetup
treats whoever last named a pair as one of its ends. So anyone who sees one datagram can use
the meetup for ever and can take an end's place (`src/meetup/README.md`; `passing-test.cc`,
`test_replay`, with the real cipher).

## 2. What it protects, and from whom

A meetup never holds a session's key: what two ends say is sealed by mosh, with a key the two
exchanged over ssh. So what someone gains by breaking into a meetup is what the meetup can do
anyway: pass datagrams for people, stop passing them (a denial, which comes with breaking into
any service), and see who talks to whom, from where, as which account (which an honest meetup
sees too, and its operator is trusted with). The owner: "so the only new thing is the ability to
help you?"

So what passes, vouchers and registrations are for is **outsiders**, who have broken into
nothing:

1. **A stranger cannot use the meetup as a relay.** That is the operator's bandwidth, bill and
   name.
2. **An outsider cannot stop one user's session**, as anyone who saw one datagram could in the
   fork, by taking an end's place.
3. **The meetup cannot be turned on anyone else**: it reflects nothing to a forged address,
   sends no answer larger than what it was sent, and does not fall over on what it is sent.

**Out of scope** (the owner): a service whose host or name has been taken over is an impostor,
another service, "and there is no threat model for not existing and being replaced by a fake";
and once the web side is broken into, "all bets are off" for that service until its operator
starts it again. The web side keeps no tokens, and nothing of a user's but the key it signs.

## 3. The shape

**gosh-meetup is one service at one name** (`meetup.cs.coloradomesa.edu`), run as two processes
under two Unix users, and a tool:

- **the web side** enrols users: they sign in with an Apple, Google or Microsoft account, and
  it signs the public key their machine made. That signature is a **pass**. It lives in a
  repository of its own (section 10).
- **the UDP side** passes datagrams for ends that show a pass and a key their user vouched for.
  It is `gosh-server meetup`, grown.
- **the term tool**, `gosh-server meetup-term`, holds the service's identity key and makes its
  terms (section 4). It may run on another machine.

What it keeps: its **identity key** and its **term keys**; on the web side, **one SQLite
database** with the web side's settings, its whitelist and its blacklist; on the UDP side,
**plain text files**, as gosh keeps all its keys and settings. It keeps no list of users and no
database of passes: a pass is checked by its signature alone.

**Where gosh keeps things.** Everything gosh itself keeps, on a user's machine or on the machine
that runs the UDP side, is plain text in gosh's own directory: `~/.config/gosh/` (or
`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/gosh/`) on Linux and BSD, `~/Library/Application Support/gosh/` on macOS,
`%APPDATA%\gosh\` on Windows. Keys are files only their owner can read.

From Tailscale's relays, as the agent remembers their published design (none of its code or
wire format is used): nothing reusable crosses the wire in the clear; an end proves it holds a
private key, afresh; a datagram is passed to a key, not to whoever spoke last. From WireGuard,
likewise from memory: a cheap check that a sender knows the relay's public key before any
costly work. From Let's Encrypt: keys that last 90 days and roll by themselves, from cron, made
well ahead, so that nothing has to be revoked and a missed run costs nothing.

**Carried in:** TweetNaCl (public domain), for the box, the signature and the hash, used when
an end enrols or registers, never on a relayed datagram. SQLite is the web side's, not gosh's.

**Not answered:** the STUN question ("yes, drop it"). REGISTERED and KEPT tell an end where it
appears to be; path-finding asks public STUN servers; and the pass names the service, so no end
finds a meetup by its answer.

**A session through the meetup has fallen back** (the owner: "meetup counts as fallen back"): it
keeps its ssh connection, path-finding goes on running, and so `--stun=none` and `--punch=never`
keep it off the meetup as they keep it from every other server (`REQ1/PATH-7`); `REQ1/PATH-2`'s
closing of ssh is for a session back on its own UDP.

## 4. Keys, and terms

| Key | Kind | Private half held by | Lives | Public half known to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the service's **identity key**, I | signature | the term tool alone, under its own Unix user or on another machine | as long as the service | every end that enrols, which pins it under the service's name |
| a term's **signing key** | signature | the web side alone, in a file only it can read | destroyed when the next term begins: 90 days | the UDP side, and every end that enrols |
| a term's **box key** | box | the UDP side alone, in a file only it can read | destroyed when its span ends | every end that enrols |
| a **user's key**, U | signature | the user, in a file only they can read; never leaves their machine | until the user makes a new one | the service, which signs it |
| a **session end's key**, E | box | one end, in memory, for one session | one session | the other end (over ssh), and the UDP side |
| a registration's key, `Kr` | mosh's cipher | one end and the UDP side | one registration | — |

**The identity key** signs nothing but the **term list** (below), and only when a term begins or
is ended early: about four times a year. The owner: "the key role means it does not have to be
[online]". An end pins it, under the service's name, the first time it takes a pass from that
service (section 6, step 6), and never takes a term the identity key has not signed. One machine
may hold passes for several services, each with its own pin.

**Terms.** A **term** is a signing key and a box key. Each is made when the one before it
begins, so one is always waiting: "keep a future key valid (so 5 max, 1 in the future)". A term
has an **id**, the first eight bytes of the hash of its two public keys, and a **span**: from the
instant it begins until 360 days after, in UTC seconds. The terms begin 90 days apart, so four are
in their span at any time, and a fifth waits. A pass carries no date of its own: "a pass does
not, the key does." It is good while its term is in its span and has not been ended early.

Only the current term's signing key is ever used, so only it is kept; a break-in can mint passes
only under it. Box keys are kept for the life of the passes that seal to them.

**The term list** is the identity key's signature over, in fixed slots: `"gosh terms v1"` (13
bytes); the service name (a slot of 256); when the list was made (8, UTC seconds); five term
slots of 88 bytes (id 8, signing key 32, box key 32, span's start 8, span's end 8; an empty one is
zeros); and sixteen slots of 8 for the ids of terms ended early, while their spans have not run
out. With its signature, 909 bytes. Of two lists, the one made later is newer.

**Making terms.** `gosh-server meetup-term`, run by whoever runs the service, by hand or every
day from cron, as certbot is; on most days it does nothing:

1. **It checks its clock first** (the owner: "roughtime is signed, and we have a clock"). It asks
   three public Roughtime servers for the time, checks each answer's signature against that
   server's published key, and goes on only if at least two agree with its own clock to within
   ten minutes. Otherwise it does nothing and says why. Days do not matter in a 90-day window;
   this is only so that a clock set wrong does not end or begin terms early. Nothing else in
   gosh asks for the time, and no end does (`REQ1/PATH-7`).
2. When the waiting term's span begins: it makes the next one, signs a new term list, destroys
   the signing key of the term before, and the box key of any term whose span has ended. Each
   private half is written to a file readable only by the side that uses it, and the public
   halves and the term list to a third that both can read; each written whole, then renamed.
3. The UDP side looks at the public file every few seconds (its change time; nothing more),
   loads what is new, and writes a mark that it has. The web side signs with the new term only
   once the mark is there, so no pass is ever signed that the UDP side cannot check.
4. It exits non-zero, saying what, on any step it could not do.

Since the next term is made 90 days before it is needed, a tool that has not run for weeks
costs nothing, and the identity key can be kept on another machine and brought out once a term.

A UDP side that has no term in its span blames itself: it answers nothing, and says so in its
log, rather than telling every end its pass is no good.

`gosh-server meetup-term --now` is the **emergency**: every term ended early in a new term list,
their private halves destroyed, and a new term at once, with a new one waiting. Ends learn it from
the next list they are sent. Everyone signs in again. If the sixteen slots for ended terms would
overflow (more than three emergencies in a year), it refuses and says to make a new identity.
It has a test.

**If the identity key itself is lost or taken**, the service needs a new identity, and every user
runs `gosh --meetup-forget NAME` and enrols again.

**Backups** hold public halves and the database, never a private key. A lost key directory is an
emergency.

**The pass**: the current term's signature over these 564 bytes, in this order:

```
"gosh pass v1"         12 bytes
service name           a slot of 256 bytes: one byte of length, then the name, then zeros
term id                8 bytes
U                      32 bytes
account                a slot of 256 bytes: provider ":" address, likewise
```

With its signature, 628 bytes. *Deferred by the owner, agreed in principle: the rates and limits
an account is allowed may be written into the pass (section 7.3).*

An account name longer than 255 bytes is refused, never cut short. Test vectors for this
encoding, and for the term list's, are shared by both repositories
([SE1/T11](../vendor/se1/guide/06-testing.md#t11)).

**A voucher**: the user's signature with `U` over `"gosh end v1"`, the service name, the term id,
`E` and `P`: "at this service, under that pass, end `E` may be passed to and from end `P`". The
client end makes both its session's vouchers, its own and the server end's, **once each per
session**; the server end sends its `E` over ssh and gets its voucher back, and never holds the
user's private key. A voucher is for one pair at one service: of use only while `P`'s private
key exists, which is one session.

**What an end seals to.** Everything an end sends while registering is boxed to its pass's
term's box key, so the pass, `U` and the account cross the network sealed.

## 5. Who is admitted: the hook, and the lists

**In gosh, the UDP side asks a hook** about every account it is shown, whatever its domain:
"hook is everywhere, just most are answered". Its contract:

```
admit( account, U )  ->  admit | refuse( why )
```

- It answers at once, from what it holds in memory; it never reads a file, a database or the
  network while it is asked. gosh tells it to load again when its files change.
- It keeps a counter that goes up whenever its answers may have changed. The UDP side compares
  the counter on every PASS and KEEP (one comparison), and asks the hook again for that
  registration when it has moved. So a user put out is put out within seconds.
- Any failure in answering is "refuse"; a failure in loading keeps what it had, and says so.
- gosh checks the account's form (below) before the hook is asked.
- **gosh's own hook answers "no"** to everyone ("this one can default to 'no'"). A service that
  admits anyone does so because its own build provides a hook that says so. gosh's tests use a
  hook of their own that admits one test account (`REQ15/PATH-4`).

**A service's own build provides its hook** (the web side's repository), as gosh's seam gives the
core its turns. So gosh carries no regular-expression engine, no database and no list format of
its own. A hook that applies the whitelist as well as the blacklist puts out the holders of
passes already given when a whitelist entry is removed or runs out; one that applies only the
blacklist stops only new enrolment.

**On the web side**, both lists are sets of regular expressions in its SQLite database, matched
with SQLite's `REGEXP` (its extension `ext/misc/regexp.c`: *from the agent's memory, to be
checked against its source, an automaton whose time is bounded by the length of the text times
the length of the expression*). Rules both sides rely on:

- an expression is matched against the whole text (`school\.edu` does not match
  `myschool.edu.example`);
- **the account's form**: lower-cased; anything outside printable ASCII, a space or a control
  character refused, before anything is signed or matched;
- the whitelist decides who may enrol; out of the box, `(apple|google|microsoft):.*`;
- a whitelist of a school's domain is checked against what the provider says the account's
  organisation is (section 6, step 3), not only against the address's suffix;
- **every entry, in either list, carries the instant it stops applying** (the owner: "so you
  don't have to remember to remove them after 4 years"), and one past it is not matched. A
  blacklist entry is added for a year unless said otherwise: no pass it could match lasts longer.

Under the default whitelist anyone with an account at the three providers can enrol, and a
blacklisted person can make a new account in a minute. **The blacklist means something only
behind a narrow whitelist.**

**No stable id; `sub` noted and left out** (the owner: "no such animal", then "note sub but
leave it out"). Every sign-in carries the provider's own id for the account, `sub`, which does
not change when the account's address does, and is not given to whoever gets the address next.
A pass that carried it would let one entry name one person exactly, whatever their address.
Without it, an address given to someone else is, to the hook, the same person.

## 6. Enrolling (on the web)

The owner, of a key carried in an address that anyone could craft: "back to paste in public key
generated by gosh." So the key goes in, and the pass comes out, by the user's own hand, and gosh
listens on nothing.

```
gosh --meetup-key          prints this machine's public key, and its fingerprint
gosh --meetup-key --new    makes a new key pair, for a key that was stolen or lost
gosh --meetup-pass [FILE]  reads a pass pasted in (or from FILE), checks it, and keeps it
gosh --meetup-forget NAME  forgets the identity pinned for that service, and its pass
```

(Options, not words after `gosh`, so that none can be taken for a host's name.)

1. `gosh --meetup-key`, the first time, makes the user's key pair and writes the private half to
   a file only the user can read (created anew, never overwritten but by `--new`, refused if
   anyone else can read it). It prints the public half, as text to copy, and its
   **fingerprint**: six words from a list of 2,048 (66 bits). `--new` keeps the old key beside
   the new one until the user's next pass is taken.
2. The user opens the service's page, signs in, and pastes the public half into it. The web side
   asks the provider to have the user **choose the account** every time (`prompt=select_account`),
   with its own `state`, an OpenID `nonce`, and PKCE, and asks for nothing but `openid email`.
3. When the provider sends the user back, the web side checks, refusing on any failure:
   - the ID token's signature, against the provider's published keys;
   - `iss` is exactly the provider's; `aud` is the service's own client id; `exp` and `iat` are
     in range; `nonce` is the one it sent; its own `state` matches;
   - `email_verified` is true (Apple may send it as the text "true": *from memory*);
   - **Google:** a whitelist of a domain is checked against the `hd` claim, not the address;
   - **Microsoft:** a whitelist of a domain is met only if the optional claim `xms_edov` is true
     (the address's domain is verified as the tenant's own) or the tenant `tid` is that domain's
     own, listed with it; the consumer tenant never meets a domain;
   - **Apple:** the reply comes as a form posted across sites, so the web side keeps its session
     on its own side and not in a cookie such a post would not carry;
   - `U` is not one of the eight small-order points, nor a non-canonical encoding;
   - the account matches the whitelist and not the blacklist.
4. **A confirmation page**: "You are enrolling a machine for `google:alice@school.edu`, whose
   key's fingerprint is:" and the six words of what was pasted. "Only continue if these are the
   words your own `gosh --meetup-key` printed."
5. The page shows the pass as text, with: "Paste this only into `gosh --meetup-pass` on your own
   machine." The text is `gosh-pass-v1:` and then base64url of the pass with the fields it signs
   (628 bytes), the term list (909) and the identity key's public half (32): 2,092 characters, in
   lines of 64.
6. `gosh --meetup-pass` reads it until a blank line, or from FILE, ignoring white space (a
   terminal on macOS cuts one line at 1,024 characters: *from memory*). It checks, and refuses
   saying which:
   - the identity key: the first time for this service's name, it is **pinned** under that name;
     afterwards it must be the pinned one;
   - the term list: signed by that identity key, for this service;
   - the pass: signed by a term in that list, in its span by this machine's clock and not ended
     early, over this service and **this machine's own `U`**; `U` not small-order.

   Then it keeps the pass beside the user's key, under the service's name. A new pass does not
   replace one that is kept until it has once been taken by the service (REGISTERED).

The first pin is taken on trust, from the page of the service the user signed in to. The web
side's request log is off for its enrolment pages and callbacks, and it sends
`Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.

**Renewing.** An end whose pass's term ends within 30 days says so on every start, "your gosh pass
for NAME expires on DATE: run `gosh --meetup-key` and sign in again", going by the service's time
from its last REGISTERED or KEPT, or by its own clock if it has none. No time server is asked.

## 7. Registering and passing (by UDP)

UDP to the service's one port. Each kind of datagram begins with a mark of its own (8 bytes) and
a version (1), and has **one exact length**, but PASS and PASSED, which carry a datagram: every
field that varies has a slot of fixed size. **Lengths are checked before anything is opened**,
the cipher's included: it guards its buffers with an `assert` that some builds leave out. `M`
below is the box key of the end's pass's term.

### 7.1 Registering (two round trips, for an end the service does not know)

```
end -> meetup   HELLO       mark | version | term id (8) | E (32) | nonce (24) | gate (16) | zeros   943 bytes
meetup -> end   COOKIE      mark | version | the HELLO's nonce (24) | cookie (24)                       57
             or NO          mark | version | the HELLO's nonce (24) | the term list (909)              942
end -> meetup   REGISTER    mark | version | term id (8) | E (32) | nonce (24) | cookie (24) | boxed E -> M:
                                { kind, version, the cookie again, P (32), the pass and the
                                  fields it signs (628), the voucher (64) }                             863
meetup -> end   REGISTERED  mark | version | nonce (24) | boxed M -> E:
                                { kind, version, the cookie again, id (8), Kr (16),
                                  where the end appears to be: family (1), address (16), port (2),
                                  the service's time (8), the term list (909) }                      1,035
        or      REFUSED     mark | version | nonce (24) | boxed M -> E:
                                { kind, version, the cookie again, why (1) }                            76
```

Cheapest first, and where it stops:

1. **HELLO.** `gate`, the first 16 bytes of the hash of a label, **the identity key's public
   half**, `E` and the nonce, is checked first: one hash. If it does not check, no answer and no
   state.
2. **NO.** If the term is not in the term list, in its span and not ended early, the service
   answers NO with its term list: it has no good key for that pass. (The owner: "the server tries
   the valid keys and comes back no.") The list is sent as the tool made it, so a NO costs no
   signature, and it is shorter than the HELLO it answers.
3. **COOKIE**, in the clear: the UDP side's own time since it started (never the time of day,
   never backward) and a tag, under a secret it makes afresh each start, over that time, the
   HELLO's source address and `E`. Good for ten seconds, at that address, for that `E`.
4. **REGISTER.** The cookie is checked first (one hash). Then a limit on costly opens per
   address, and **a limit on costly opens for the whole service** (a token bucket; passing
   datagrams is served first). Only then the box, with the key agreement done once and kept
   with the registration, if one is made, and dropped if not; then the two signatures; then the
   hook (section 5).
5. Taken if: `P` is not `E`; the pass is the term's signature over this service, the term, `U`,
   and the account; the hook admits the account and `U`; and the voucher is `U`'s signature over
   this service, the term, `E` and `P`. It makes a **registration**: `E` at that address, for
   `P`, of that account, under that term until its span ends, with a random `id` and a new `Kr`.
   An `E` registered already: an older cookie is ignored; the same cookie gets the same answer
   again.
6. After a good cookie, whatever fails is answered REFUSED with why (the hook does not admit them;
   the service is full; the user's registrations are over their limit). Before one, nothing but
   NO.

The end takes a COOKIE or NO only if it carries the nonce of a HELLO it has outstanding, and a
REGISTERED or REFUSED only if it carries the cookie it sent. Each side checks the direction bit
of the nonce it receives. A box whose shared secret is all zero is refused.

**What an end keeps from a term list**, from NO, REGISTERED or a pass: only one signed by the
identity pinned for that service, and only if it is newer than the newest it has. It keeps the
newest, and with it which terms have been ended early.

**What an end does with NO.** A NO whose list still shows the end's term as good is ignored: it is
old, or not the service's. Otherwise the end says, every time, "NAME did not take your gosh pass:
its term ended early" or "...its term has passed", and "run `gosh --meetup-key` and sign in
again"; and it goes on trying, once a minute, in case it is wrong. A server end only reports it to
the client end over ssh.

**What an end does with silence.** After five HELLOs with no answer, it says "no answer from NAME:
it is down, or its identity has changed (ask whoever runs it)", and goes on trying, slowly.

### 7.2 Keeping a registration (one round trip, no public-key work)

```
end -> meetup   KEEP    mark | version | id (8) | counter (8) | sealed under Kr: { kind, version }
meetup -> end   KEPT    mark | version | counter (8)          | sealed under Kr: { kind, version,
                                                         where the end appears to be, the service's time,
                                                         the current term's id }
```

- A KEEP is taken only from the address the registration was made from, as a PASS is.
- If the registration's term has ended, or the hook no longer admits the account, the
  registration is dropped, and the next HELLO says why (NO, or REFUSED after a cookie).
- An end holds a registration while its session is fallen back and path-finding runs, and sends
  KEEP every 25 seconds, give or take a few.
- **It starts again from HELLO** when a KEEP has had no KEPT for 3.5 seconds, when it has passed
  datagrams and been passed none for 3.5 seconds, or when REGISTERED never came: after 3.5 s, 7 s,
  14 s, ... up to a minute, each varied a little. A NAT that moves an end's port is mended so.

### 7.3 Passing a datagram on

```
end -> meetup   PASS     mark | version | id (8) | counter (8) | the datagram, sealed under Kr with that counter
meetup -> end   PASSED   mark | version | the datagram
```

- In this order: find `id`; check the source address; check the length; check that the
  registration's term has not ended and the hook's counter has not moved (two comparisons); check
  the registration's and its user's rate (below); open the seal; then mark `counter` used (a
  window of 1,024). Send PASSED to the registration of the `P` this one is for, **if that one is
  for this one's `E`**, at its address, if heard within two minutes.
- Counters under one `Kr` only go up, the end's and the service's told apart by one bit; a
  replaced registration gets a new `Kr`.
- An end takes a PASSED only from the service it is registered with.
- At most 1,500 bytes of datagram; a session's are about 1,300. The cipher's buffers are 2,048.
- **Rate.** *Deferred by the owner, agreed in principle:* each registration and each account may
  pass so many datagrams and bytes a second, from what a mosh session needs, measured; perhaps
  written into the pass. Until then an admitted user has a relay with no limit but the service's
  whole.

### 7.4 How many, and who is put out

At most 2,048 registrations (about 10 MB, key agreements included), 16 for one account (and,
deferred with the rates, a limit for one sign-in domain).

- A registration whose `P` has not registered within 60 seconds is dropped: it has nobody to pass
  to.
- An account's seventeenth puts out **that account's** registration that has gone longest
  without passing a datagram to an end that is for it.
- When the service is full, a new registration puts out the registration, **of any account**,
  that has gone longest without passing a datagram. What is in use stays; what is merely held
  goes first, whoever holds it.

## 8. What the two relays say to each other over ssh

After its greeting the server end sends its `E`. The client end, **once per session**, answers
with the service's name, the term id, the server end's voucher, the pass with every field it
signs, the term list, and the identity key's public half. Each is written in base64url, since the
messages between relays are split at spaces (`src/relay/frame.h`). Old relays ignore what they do
not know. The server end checks the term list against the identity it was sent; it pins nothing.

The server end learns the user's account name and pass this way. It needs them to register, and
it is the user's own server.

## 9. Each input: absent, late, wrong, lying

- **Absent.** The service down: no COOKIE; the end's log says "no answer to hello", and after five
  it says so to the user (7.1); it goes on with its other paths, asking again. No pass on the
  machine: the end does not try, and `gosh` says to run `gosh --meetup-key` and sign in. The
  other end not registered within 60 seconds: the registration is dropped. The web side or a
  sign-in provider down: nobody can enrol or renew; passes already given go on working until
  their terms end. The term tool not run: nothing, for up to 90 days, since the next term is
  already made.
- **Late.** A cookie older than ten seconds is refused; the end asks for another. An old PASS is
  refused once 1,024 behind. A pass whose term has ended gets NO. A term list older than the
  newest an end has is ignored, and so is a NO that shows the end's term good.
- **Wrong.** Before a good cookie, nothing is answered but NO. After one: REFUSED with why, or (a
  box that does not open, a voucher that is not that user's for this service, term and pair)
  dropped. On the web: every check in section 6 that fails says, on the page, which.
- **Lying.** An end for a `P` that is not for it is passed nothing. A user can vouch for any pair
  of keys; it spends their own registrations. A provider that vouches for an address it should not
  (a tenant that sets its users' addresses itself): that is why Microsoft domains need `xms_edov`
  or their own tenant, and Google domains need `hd`. A pass made under terms the service never
  made: refused by the pinned identity. A meetup that lies about where an end appears to be, or
  about its time: the end tries a wrong address, or warns of renewal at the wrong time; nothing
  of the session is at stake.
- **The clock.** The term tool's: checked against Roughtime before it acts. The UDP side's: it is
  what decides which terms are good; if it has none in span it answers nothing. An end's: `gosh
  --meetup-pass` may refuse a good pass, or keep one whose term has ended.
- **The service's own state.** Missing or unreadable at start: it does not start. Unreadable
  later: the old state stays, and it says so.

## 10. What an attacker can do, and what is not closed

Section 2 says what is protected and what is out of scope.

| Attacker | Today | Proposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knows nothing of the service | Gets no answer | Gets no answer by UDP (a scanner); the web side is a web page, and gives the identity and term list to anyone who asks |
| Knows the service's identity; holds no pass | Uses the meetup for ever with one copied datagram; takes an end's place | Gets cookies, and NO with the public term list (shorter than the HELLO); makes the service do one hash a datagram, and a limited number of costly opens; sees that some `E` registers every 25 s, from where. Cannot register, pass, or take a place |
| On the path, can forge addresses; no pass | As above | Can replay only what the service did not receive. Can answer an end first with a false COOKIE, and so keep it from registering for a while: a denial, which dropping would give anyway. An old NO is ignored |
| Anyone with an account at the three providers | — | Enrols as themselves under the default whitelist: **most people**. Is admitted by UDP only if the service's own hook says so; gosh's own hook says no |
| A Microsoft tenant's admin who sets a user's address to someone else's, or a personal account with a school address | — | Refused for a domain's whitelist: only `xms_edov`, or the domain's own tenant |
| A former member of a school who still has a verified school address on a personal Google account | — | Refused for the school's whitelist, which is checked against `hd` |
| An address that was someone else's, given out again by the school | — | To the hook, the same person: inherits the old holder's blacklist entry and limits (`sub` would tell them apart; left out) |
| Someone who sends a victim an enrolment link for the attacker's own key | — | Nothing: no key travels in a link; the user pastes their own, past a page that shows its fingerprint |
| Someone who knows a user's key (their server host does) and hands them a pass of terms of their own | — | Refused once the user's machine has pinned the service's identity. On a machine that has never enrolled with that service, taken; the real service then answers no HELLO of it, and the user is told so after five. A denial, by someone the user handed a pass from |
| Another user of the service | Takes an end's place, if they see the pair's name | Cannot open another's messages, register as another's end, or be passed another pair's datagrams. Has their own limits; fills the service only as far as the whole service's eviction allows. Until rates are set, can relay without limit between machines of their own |
| A server host the user logged in to | Holds the shared key: a user of the meetup for ever | For that session, is that session's server end. Learns the user's account name and pass. Afterwards holds a voucher naming an end nobody is: put out after 60 seconds. Cannot relay for itself |
| Someone who steals a user's private key | — | Is that user, until the hook refuses the key or the pass's term ends; the user makes a new one with `--new` and signs in. Cannot read or join a running session |
| Someone who takes over a user's account at the provider | — | Can enrol a key of their own as that user, until the account is blacklisted |
| Someone who breaks into the web side | Sees which addresses talk and how much | "All bets are off" for that service: signs passes under the current term until an emergency; may serve its own identity to machines enrolling for the first time, which then reach nothing. Holds no box key, so reads no registration; holds no identity key |
| Someone who breaks into the UDP side | — | Holds the box keys of the last four terms: can read the registrations sealed to them (which account, at which address, for which peer, each `Kr`), pass or not pass, and pose as the service by UDP until an emergency. Holds no signing key and no identity key. Not the content of any session |
| Someone who takes the identity key | — | Can make terms, and so passes, as the service, until users forget it and enrol under a new one. It is used four times a year, and may be kept on another machine |
| Someone who takes over the service's host or name | — | An impostor: out of scope (section 2) |
| Anyone, with huge or malformed datagrams | (the fork never opened what it was sent) | Refused by length before anything is opened; what fails to open is caught |

In every row, nobody reads or changes what the two ends say: that is sealed by the session.

**Not closed, besides what the table says:**

- **No forward secrecy for registrations** (the owner: "ok"); the remedy is an emergency.
- **The first pin is taken on trust**, from the page of the service the user signed in to.
- **The service is trusted** not to drop, and sees who talks to whom, how much, and as which
  account. It writes none of that to a log (deferred by the owner).
- **A PASSED proves nothing to the end that gets it.** The session refuses what is not its own.
- **An end's key `E` is in the clear** when it registers. mosh's own datagrams carry a counter in
  the clear.
- **A NAT that changes an end's port** costs 3.5 seconds and two round trips to mend.
- **A user's private key is a file** with no passphrase.
- **The pass and the account name reach the user's server** (section 8).
- **The regular expressions are whoever runs the service's to get right.** Full-text matching and
  lower-cased ASCII remove the commonest mistakes, not all.
- **TweetNaCl** is small and slow. The service's CPU limits are to be set from TweetNaCl measured
  on the machine it will run on. Its signature check accepts some signatures in more than one
  form; nothing here is keyed on a signature's bytes.
- **IPv4.** The UDP side listens on IPv4, as today; the answer's address field fits IPv6.
- **Cost.** Each relayed datagram is sealed once more by the sender and opened once by the
  service. This path is the last resort before ssh.

## 11. What changes in gosh, and what is new

**Done already** (groundwork): `vendor/tweetnacl/`, checked against published answers in the
check; `src/meetup/nacl.*`, the one wrapper that calls it. *It does its key agreement twice per
box, once to check for an all-zero secret and once to seal; it is to do it once, and to keep the
agreed key for a registration (section 7.1, step 4).*

**In gosh** (C++, this repository):

- `src/meetup/`: `proof.*` goes, and `answer.*` (the STUN answer) with it. New logic, each with a
  test of bytes and numbers and no socket: `pass.h` (the encodings of section 4, the pass's and
  the term list's, made and checked, with the shared test vectors; small-order keys refused),
  `terms.h` (which are good at an instant, which ended early, which list is newer, the pin by
  name), `cookie.h`, `messages.h` (every kind, at its exact length), `registrations.h` (7.1, 7.2,
  7.4), `passing.h` (7.3), `admit.h` (the hook of section 5, gosh's own that says no, and the
  tests' that admits one account). `serve.cc` stays the edge: the socket, the time since start,
  gosh's directory, loading the hook.
- `gosh-server meetup-term` (with `--now`): holds the identity key and rolls terms (section 4),
  with a Roughtime client of its own (three servers, their keys carried in; TweetNaCl checks the
  signatures). Its keys and each side's are files in gosh's directory, each readable by one Unix
  user. A subcommand of `gosh-server`, as `meetup` is, so no new program (`REQ6/NAME-1`).
- `gosh --meetup-key`, `--meetup-pass` and `--meetup-forget`: section 6. Text in and out; gosh
  listens on nothing and needs no HTTPS.
- A list of 2,048 words for fingerprints, carried in, with its licence stated (to be chosen).
- What the relays say (section 8); a session through the meetup keeps its ssh connection
  (section 3).
- `src/pathfinding/`: the end's half, as logic, when that part is reshaped (the rest of queue
  item 8). **In the fork today, found by the first attack:** `punch.cc` hands any probe-marked
  datagram of up to 65,528 bytes to the cipher's `decrypt`, whose 2,048-byte buffer is guarded
  only by an `assert`. Path-finding is off in gosh; this is fixed before it is switched on.
- Tests: the attacks of 2026-11-15 turned round (each thing an onlooker could do becomes a check
  that they cannot), and one for each row of section 10 that a test can reach.
- No private key, `Kr`, pass, voucher, account name or the bytes of a registration is written
  to a log.

**New, in a repository of its own, with requirements of its own:** the web side (section 6): its
SQLite database, its regular expressions and their expiry, its sign-in, its record of enrolments
(when, which account, the whole `U`, kept a year: the owner, "ok"), and its build of the UDP side
with its own hook (section 5). It shares with gosh the encodings of section 4, by their test
vectors, and the hook's declaration.

A gosh meetup and the fork's relays ignore each other; the requirements already say gosh does
not work with the fork's releases at the other end.

## 12. What whoever runs a service does

- **Once:** register the service with each sign-in provider, asking only `openid email`; write the
  whitelist and the hook's lists; make the Unix users (web side, UDP side, and the term tool's,
  unless it runs on another machine); run `gosh-server meetup-term` to make the identity and the
  first two terms; start the two sides.
- **Every day** (cron), or once a term by hand where the identity key is kept elsewhere:
  `gosh-server meetup-term`. On most days it does nothing; it exits non-zero, and says why, if
  anything is wrong (Roughtime and its clock disagreeing, for one).
- **When someone must be put out:** add them to the blacklist, with when it ends (a year unless
  said); the hook answers the UDP side differently within seconds.
- **After a break-in of either side, or a lost key directory:** `gosh-server meetup-term --now`.
  Everyone pastes their key and signs in again.
- **After the identity key is taken or lost:** a new identity; every user runs
  `gosh --meetup-forget NAME` and enrols again.
- **Backups:** the database and the public keys. Never a private key.

## 13. How this proposal got here

- **Revision 1**: one shared key and the clock. Attacked: twelve findings (among them that
  someone with the shared key who saw one registration kept that pair from anywhere; an
  oversized datagram would abort the meetup inside the cipher; a tag over a counter alone let a
  payload be swapped; the clock rule failed silently).
- **Revision 2** took them, with a key per user derived from a master. It kept to a shared-key
  cipher because the agent read `REQ1/SHAPE-3` as ruling out public keys; **that was a mistake:**
  it rules out a library a machine may lack, not source carried here.
- **Revision 3**: key pairs, authorities that sign users' keys by hand. Attacked: eleven findings
  (a voucher bound to nothing let a server host lock a user out for good; every stranger's
  datagram cost a costly step; answers were not tied to their requests; renewing with signatures
  would not fit; silence for every failure). **Revision 4** took those.
- **Revision 5**: the owner's direction: one service that signs its own passes, with term keys
  that roll, sign-in with an account the user has. Attacked: thirteen findings, among them that
  **anyone could have enrolled as anyone** (the design trusted the provider's address without
  naming which claim or check), a crafted link could enrol an attacker's key, and the signing key
  was not kept from the UDP side. **Revision 6** took those; SQLite and regular expressions are
  the web side's, and gosh keeps plain text.
- **Revision 7**: the owner's answers to revision 6: a hook; NO; terms good by a signed date
  (Roughtime from the service); enrolment by pasting. Attacked: thirteen findings, the worst that
  **a term vouched only for itself**, so a pass under made-up terms passed every check, and that
  Roughtime from the service added nothing while bringing a new master key and an amplifier.
- **Revision 8**: an identity key that signs the terms, pinned; no Roughtime; NO gated and never
  recorded; the hook's contract and gosh's own "no"; fixed slots; six words; expiring list
  entries. Attacked: fifteen findings. The worst: a break-in on the web side could serve its own
  identity to a first enrolment; the "append-only" blacklist was not; a list newer by its date
  could be poisoned by a clock; the identity key was online daily for nothing; the meetup was not
  squared with `REQ1/PATH-2` and `REQ1/PATH-7`. And the sizes were wrong.
- **Revision 9** (this): the owner changed the picture (section 2): what a break-in gains is what
  the service can do anyway, so the design guards against outsiders and leaves an impostor and a
  broken-in web side out of scope. So the service's words and the append-only file are gone; the
  pin is by name, on trust; the term tool checks its clock against public Roughtime ("roughtime is
  signed, and we have a clock"); terms are made a term ahead, five at most, and the identity key
  signs only when a term begins or ends, and may be kept elsewhere; a session through the meetup
  has fallen back ("meetup counts as fallen back"). From the fifth attack also: a NO showing the
  pass good is ignored; silence is explained; KEEP checks the address; the hook's counter is
  compared on PASS; Microsoft domains need their own tenant; `--new`; small-order keys refused;
  options in place of words after `gosh`; `gosh-server meetup-term` in place of a new program;
  sizes computed. **Not yet attacked.**
