**A proposal, not the design in force. Revision 8, 2026-11-15.** Nothing here is built yet,
but for its groundwork (TweetNaCl carried in, and gosh's wrapper of it: section 10). The
requirements it is written to are `REQUIREMENTS_v15.md` as rewritten the same day, not yet
accepted.

## 1. What is wrong today

A meetup has one key, shared by everyone allowed to use it. To have a datagram passed on, an
end puts twelve bytes in front of it: four random bytes and a tag of those four bytes. They
travel in the clear, say nothing of who sent them, when, or for which pair, and the meetup
treats whoever last named a pair as one of its ends. So anyone who sees one datagram can use
the meetup for ever and can take an end's place (`src/meetup/README.md`; `passing-test.cc`,
`test_replay`, with the real cipher).

## 2. The shape

**gosh-meetup is one service at one address**, run as two processes under two Unix users, and a
tool under a third:

- **the web side** enrols users: they sign in with an Apple, Google or Microsoft account, and
  it signs the public key their machine made. That signature is a **pass**. It lives in a
  repository of its own (section 10).
- **the UDP side** passes datagrams for ends that show a pass and a key their user vouched for.
  It is what `gosh-server meetup` is today, grown.
- **the term tool**, `gosh-meetup term`, holds the service's identity key and makes its terms
  (section 3).

What it keeps: its **identity key** and its **term keys** (section 3); on the web side, **one
SQLite database** with the web side's settings, its whitelist and its blacklist; on the UDP side,
**plain text files**, as gosh keeps all its keys and settings. It keeps no list of users and no
database of passes: a pass is checked by its signature alone.

**Where gosh keeps things.** Everything gosh itself keeps, on a user's machine or on the machine
that runs the UDP side, is plain text in gosh's own directory: `~/.config/gosh/` (or
`$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/gosh/`) on Linux and BSD, `~/Library/Application Support/gosh/` on macOS,
`%APPDATA%\gosh\` on Windows. Keys are files only their owner can read.

From Tailscale's relays, as the agent remembers their published design (none of its code or
wire format is used): nothing reusable crosses the wire in the clear; an end proves it holds a
private key, afresh; a datagram is passed to a key, not to whoever spoke last. From WireGuard,
likewise from memory: a cheap check that a sender knows the relay's public key before any
costly work.

**Carried in:** TweetNaCl (public domain), for the box, the signature and the hash, used when
an end enrols or registers, never on a relayed datagram. SQLite is the web side's, not gosh's.
**Not used:** Roughtime, or any time server (the owner, on the fourth attack; section 12). **Not
answered:** the STUN question. The fork's meetup answered it, and ends found meetups that way; now
the pass names the service, REGISTERED and KEPT say where an end appears to be, and path-finding
asks public STUN servers. The owner: "yes, drop it".

## 3. Keys, and terms

| Key | Kind | Private half held by | Lives | Public half known to |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the service's **identity key**, I | signature | the term tool alone, under its own Unix user | as long as the service | every end that enrols, which pins it |
| a term's **signing key** | signature | the web side alone, in a file only it can read | destroyed when the next term begins: 90 days | the UDP side, and every end that enrols |
| a term's **box key** | box | the UDP side alone, in a file only it can read | destroyed when its span ends: 360 days | every end that enrols |
| a **user's key**, U | signature | the user, in a file only they can read; never leaves their machine | as long as the user likes | the service, which signs it |
| a **session end's key**, E | box | one end, in memory, for one session | one session | the other end (over ssh), and the UDP side |
| a registration's key, `Kr` | mosh's cipher | one end and the UDP side | one registration | — |

**The identity key** is the root of everything an end believes about the service. It signs
nothing but the **term list** (below), and only the term tool holds it, under a Unix user of its
own that neither side runs as. An end pins it the first time it takes a pass (section 5, step
6), and never takes a term the identity key has not signed.

**Terms.** Every 90 days the tool makes a **term**: a signing key and a box key. A term has an
**id**, the first eight bytes of the hash of its two public keys, and a **span**: the instant
it begins and the instant, 360 days later, when its passes stop being good, both in UTC seconds.
A pass carries no date of its own: "a pass does not, the key does." It is good while its term is
in its span and not ended early. Four terms are in their span at any time.

Only the current term's signing key is ever used, so only it is kept; a break-in can mint passes
only under it. Box keys are kept for the life of the passes that seal to them.

**The term list** is the identity key's signature over, in fixed slots: `"gosh terms v1"`; the
service name; when it was made (UTC seconds); for each of up to five terms (four in their span and
one about to begin), its id, its two public keys and its span; and the ids of up to eight terms
ended early. About 650 bytes. The tool makes a new one whenever it makes a term or ends one, and
once a day besides. The UDP side sends it to ends in REGISTERED and NO (section 6.1); the web
side sends it with every pass.

**Making terms.** `gosh-meetup term`, run by whoever runs the service (by hand, or every day
from cron; on most days it does only the daily term list):

1. A term's keys are made seven days before its span begins. Each private half is written to a
   file readable only by the side that uses it, and the public halves and the new term list to a
   third that both can read; each written whole, then renamed.
2. The UDP side looks at the public file every few seconds (its change time; nothing more), loads
   a new term and its private box key, and writes a mark that it has.
3. The web side signs with a new term only once its span has begun and the UDP side's mark is
   there. So no pass is ever signed that the UDP side cannot check.
4. **The clock.** The tool keeps the latest time it has acted at. If the machine's clock is now
   before it, or more than two days past the time it expected to run next, it refuses to make,
   end or destroy anything, and says why; `gosh-meetup term --clock-ok` is how the operator says
   the clock is right after looking. Its first run, with nothing kept, makes the identity key and
   the first term and destroys nothing.
5. It exits non-zero, saying what, on any step it could not do.

A UDP side that has no term in its span blames itself: it answers nothing at all, and says so in
its log, rather than telling every end its pass is no good.

`gosh-meetup term --now` is the **emergency**: a new term at once, every other term ended early
in the term list, and their private halves destroyed. Ends learn it from the next term list
they are sent, and say so (section 6.1). Everyone signs in again. It has a test.

**If the identity key itself is lost or taken**, the service is a new service: users run
`gosh meetup-pass --forget` and enrol again, and see the new identity's words. That is said in
section 9 and section 11.

**Backups** hold public halves and the database, never a private key. A lost key directory is an
emergency.

**The pass**: the current term's signature over, in this order:

```
"gosh pass v1"         12 bytes
service name           a slot of 256 bytes: one byte of length, then the name, then zeros
term id                8 bytes
U                      32 bytes
account                a slot of 256 bytes: provider ":" address, likewise
```

*Deferred by the owner, agreed in principle: the rates and limits an account is allowed may be
written into the pass, so that the service need not look them up (section 6.4).*

An account name longer than 255 bytes is refused, never cut short. Test vectors for this exact
encoding, and for the term list's, are shared by both repositories
([SE1/T11](../vendor/se1/guide/06-testing.md#t11)). *No stable id beside the account name: the
owner's answer (section 4).*

**A voucher**: the user's signature with `U` over `"gosh end v1"`, the service name, the term id,
`E` and `P`: "at this service, under that pass, end `E` may be passed to and from end `P`". The
client end makes both its session's vouchers, its own and the server end's, **once each per
session**; the server end sends its `E` over ssh and gets its voucher back, and never holds the
user's private key. A voucher is for one pair at one service: of use only while `P`'s private
key exists, which is one session.

**What an end seals to.** Everything an end sends while registering is boxed to its pass's
term's box key, so the pass, `U` and the account cross the network sealed.

## 4. Who is admitted: the hook, and the lists

**In gosh, the UDP side asks a hook** about every account it is shown, whatever its domain, at
every registration and every keep: "hook is everywhere, just most are answered". Its contract:

```
admit( account, U )  ->  admit | refuse( why )
```

- It answers at once, from what it holds in memory; it never reads a file, a database or the
  network while it is asked. It is told to load again when gosh sees its files change.
- It keeps a counter that goes up whenever its answers may have changed. The UDP side asks again,
  for a registration it holds, only when the counter has moved, never on a datagram.
- Any failure, in loading or in answering, is "refuse".
- gosh checks the account's form (section 5's rules) before the hook is asked.
- **gosh's own default answers "no"** to everyone ("this one can default to 'no'"). A service
  that admits anyone does so because its own build provides a hook that says so.

**A service's own build provides its hook** (the web side's repository), as gosh's seam gives the
core its turns. So gosh carries no regular-expression engine, no database and no list format of
its own. What that build is asked to do, because gosh relies on it:

- What the hook loads is written by the operator, or **added to** by the web side: the web side
  can put someone on the blacklist within seconds, and cannot take anyone off it (an append-only
  file the hook reads; a file that has grown shorter is a fault the hook reports, and it keeps
  what it had). So a break-in on the web side cannot let back in someone the operator put out.

**On the web side**, both lists are sets of regular expressions in its SQLite database, matched
with SQLite's `REGEXP` (its extension `ext/misc/regexp.c`: *from the agent's memory, to be
checked against its source, an automaton whose time is bounded by the length of the text times
the length of the expression*). Rules both sides rely on:

- an expression is matched against the whole text (`school\.edu` does not match
  `myschool.edu.example`);
- the account name is lower-cased, and anything outside printable ASCII, a space or a control
  character is refused, before anything is signed or matched;
- the whitelist decides who may enrol; out of the box, `(apple|google|microsoft):.*`;
- a whitelist of a school's domain is checked against what the provider says the account's
  organisation is (section 5, step 3), not only against the address's suffix;
- **every entry, in either list, carries the instant it stops applying** (the owner: "so you
  don't have to remember to remove them after 4 years"), and one past it is not matched, and is
  removed by itself. A blacklist entry is added for a year unless said otherwise: no pass it
  could match lasts longer.

Under the web side's default whitelist anyone with an account at the three providers can enrol,
and a blacklisted person can make a new account in a minute. **The blacklist means something only
behind a narrow whitelist.** Section 9 says so.

**No stable id; `sub` noted and left out** (the owner: "no such animal", then "note sub but
leave it out"). Every sign-in carries the provider's own id for the account, `sub`, which does
not change when the account's address does, and is not given to whoever gets the address next.
A pass that carried it would let one entry name one person exactly, whatever their address.
Without it, an address given to someone else is, to the hook, the same person (section 9).

## 5. Enrolling (on the web)

The owner, of a key carried in an address that anyone could craft: "back to paste in public key
generated by gosh." So the key goes in, and the pass comes out, by the user's own hand, and gosh
listens on nothing.

```
gosh meetup-key                      prints this machine's public key, and its six words
gosh meetup-pass                     reads a pass pasted in, checks it, and keeps it
gosh meetup-pass --forget            forgets the service's identity and pass (section 3)
```

1. `gosh meetup-key`, the first time, makes the user's key pair and writes the private half to a
   file only the user can read (created anew, never overwritten, refused if anyone else can read
   it). It prints the public half, as text to copy, and its fingerprint as **six words** from a
   list of 2,048 (66 bits).
2. The user opens the service's page, signs in, and pastes the public half into it. The web side
   asks the provider to have the user **choose the account** every time (`prompt=select_account`),
   with its own `state`, an OpenID `nonce`, and PKCE.
3. When the provider sends the user back, the web side checks, refusing on any failure:
   - the ID token's signature, against the provider's published keys;
   - `iss` is exactly the provider's; `aud` is the service's own client id; `exp` and `iat` are
     in range; `nonce` is the one it sent; its own `state` matches;
   - `email_verified` is true;
   - **Google:** a whitelist of a domain is checked against the `hd` claim, not the address;
   - **Microsoft:** the address is taken only if the optional claim `xms_edov` is true (the
     address's domain is verified as the tenant's own), or if the tenant `tid` is on a list the
     service keeps; personal accounts carry the one fixed consumer `tid`;
   - **Apple:** the reply comes as a form posted across sites, so the web side keeps its session
     on its own side and not in a cookie such a post would not carry;
   - the account matches the whitelist and not the blacklist.
4. **A confirmation page**: "You are enrolling a machine for `google:alice@school.edu`, whose key's
   words are:" and the six words of what was pasted. "Only continue if these are the words your
   own `gosh meetup-key` printed." It shows the service's identity too, as six words of its own.
5. The page shows the pass as text, in large type with: "Nobody legitimate will ever ask you for
   this. Paste it only into `gosh meetup-pass` on your own machine." The text is
   `gosh-pass-v1:` and then base64url of the pass, the fields it signs, the term list and the
   identity key's public half; about 1,400 characters, broken into lines of 64.
6. `gosh meetup-pass` reads it until a blank line, or from a file named on its command line,
   ignoring white space (a terminal on macOS cuts one line at 1,024 characters). It checks, and
   refuses saying which:
   - the identity key: the first time, it is **pinned**, and its six words printed for the user
     to compare with any the service publishes elsewhere; afterwards, it must be the pinned one;
   - the term list: signed by that identity key, for this service;
   - the pass: signed by a term in that list, in its span by this machine's clock and not ended
     early, over this service and **this machine's own `U`**.

   Then it keeps the pass beside the user's key. A new pass does not replace one that is kept
   until it has once been taken by the service (REGISTERED).

A crafted link can no longer carry an attacker's key: the key is what the user pastes. Someone
who talks a user into pasting the attacker's key, past a page that names the user's account and
words that are not their own, still can; the warnings are there for that. Someone who hands a
user a pass of terms they made themselves is refused once the identity is pinned; on a machine
that has never enrolled, the first identity is taken on trust (section 9).

The web side turns off its server's request log for its enrolment pages and callbacks, and sends
`Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.

**Renewing.** REGISTERED and KEPT carry the service's time, sealed. An end whose pass's term ends
within 30 days of it says, on every start, "your gosh pass expires on DATE: run `gosh meetup-key`
and sign in again". No time server is asked.

## 6. Registering and passing (by UDP)

UDP to the service's one port. Each kind of datagram has a mark of its own (8 bytes), a version,
and **one exact length**, but the two that carry a datagram: every field that varies has a slot of
fixed size, padded. **Lengths are checked before anything is opened**, the cipher's included: it
guards its buffer with an `assert` that some builds leave out. `M` below is the box key of the
end's pass's term.

### 6.1 Registering (two round trips, for an end the service does not know)

```
end -> meetup   HELLO       mark | term id (8) | E (32) | nonce (24) | gate (16) | zeros, to NO's length
meetup -> end   COOKIE      mark | the HELLO's nonce (24) | cookie (24)
             or NO          mark | the HELLO's nonce (24) | the term list
end -> meetup   REGISTER    mark | term id (8) | E (32) | nonce (24) | cookie (24) | boxed E -> M:
                                { kind, version, the cookie again, P (32), the pass and the
                                  fields it signs (section 3), the voucher (64) }
meetup -> end   REGISTERED  mark | nonce (24) | boxed M -> E:
                                { kind, version, the cookie again, id (8), Kr (16),
                                  where the end appears to be: family (1), address (16), port (2),
                                  the service's time (8), the term list }
        or      REFUSED     mark | nonce (24) | boxed M -> E: { kind, version, the cookie again, why (1) }
```

Cheapest first, and where it stops:

1. **HELLO.** `gate`, the first 16 bytes of the hash of a label, **the identity key's public
   half**, `E` and the nonce, is checked first: one hash. If it does not check, no answer and no
   state. So only someone who knows the service's identity is answered at all.
2. **NO.** If the term is not in the term list, in its span and not ended early, the service
   answers NO with its current term list: it has no good key for that pass. (The owner: "the
   server tries the valid keys and comes back no.") The term list is made by the tool and sent as
   it is, so a NO costs no signature, and it is no longer than the HELLO it answers.
3. **COOKIE**, in the clear: the UDP side's own time since it started (never the time of day,
   never backward) and a tag, under a secret it makes afresh each start, over that time, the
   HELLO's source address and `E`. Good for ten seconds, at that address, for that `E`.
4. **REGISTER.** The cookie is checked first (one hash). Then a limit on costly opens per
   address, and **a limit on costly opens for the whole service** (a token bucket; passing
   datagrams is served first). Only then the box, with the key agreement done once for this `E`
   and kept for the answer; then the two signatures; then the hook (section 4), if its counter
   has moved since it last answered for this account and key.
5. Taken if: `P` is not `E`; the pass is the term's signature over this service, the term, `U`,
   and the account; the hook admits the account and `U`; and the voucher is `U`'s signature over
   this service, the term, `E` and `P`. It makes a **registration**: `E` at that address, for
   `P`, of that account, under that term until its span ends, with a random `id` and a new `Kr`.
   An `E` registered already: an older cookie is ignored; the same cookie gets the same answer
   again.
6. After a good cookie, whatever fails is answered REFUSED with why (the hook does not admit them;
   the service is full; the user's registrations are over their limit). Before one, nothing but
   NO.

The end takes a COOKIE or NO only if it carries the nonce of a HELLO it has outstanding, and a
REGISTERED or REFUSED only if it carries the cookie it sent. Each side checks the direction bit
of the nonce it receives. A box whose shared secret is all zero is refused.

**What an end does with NO.** It checks the term list's signature against its pinned identity,
and takes no NO whose list does not check. A NO is not tied to the HELLO beyond its nonce, so
someone who sees the HELLO could send an old list back; so **an end never records a NO**: it
keeps its pass, tries again slowly (once a minute), and says, every time, "the service did not
take your gosh pass (it has expired, or the service's keys changed): run gosh meetup-key and sign
in again", naming which if the list says (the term ended early; its span has passed). A server end
only reports it to the client end over ssh. A term list that ends terms early is kept, and every
pass under them is shown as ended.

### 6.2 Keeping a registration (one round trip, no public-key work)

```
end -> meetup   KEEP    mark | id (8) | counter (8) | sealed under Kr: { kind, version }
meetup -> end   KEPT    mark | counter (8)          | sealed under Kr: { kind, version,
                                                         where the end appears to be, the service's time,
                                                         the current term's id }
```

- If the registration's term has ended, or the hook no longer admits the account, the
  registration is dropped and the next HELLO says why (NO, or REFUSED after a cookie).
- An end holds a registration for as long as path-finding is running and sends KEEP every 25
  seconds, give or take a few.
- **It starts again from HELLO** when a KEEP or a PASS has had nothing back for 3.5 seconds, when
  KEPT shows its address has changed, or when REGISTERED never came: after 3.5 s, 7 s, 14 s, ...
  up to a minute, each varied a little.

### 6.3 Passing a datagram on

```
end -> meetup   PASS     mark | id (8) | counter (8) | the datagram, sealed under Kr with that counter
meetup -> end   PASSED   mark | the datagram
```

- In this order: find `id`; check the source address; check the length; check that the
  registration's term has not ended (one comparison, so a span's end is kept to the second); check
  the registration's and its user's rate (below); open the seal; then mark `counter` used (a
  window of 1,024). Send PASSED to the registration of the `P` this one is for, **if that one is
  for this one's `E`**, at its address, if heard within two minutes.
- Counters under one `Kr` only go up, the end's and the service's told apart by one bit; a
  replaced registration gets a new `Kr`.
- An end takes a PASSED only from the service it is registered with.
- At most 2,032 bytes of datagram; a session's are about 1,300.
- **Rate.** *Deferred by the owner, agreed in principle:* each registration and each account may
  pass so many datagrams and bytes a second, from what a mosh session needs, measured; perhaps
  written into the pass.

### 6.4 How many, and who is put out

At most 2,048 registrations (about 10 MB), 16 for one account (and, deferred with the rates, a
limit for one sign-in domain).

- A registration whose `P` has not registered within 60 seconds is dropped: it has nobody to pass
  to.
- An account's seventeenth puts out **that account's** registration that has gone longest
  without passing a datagram to an end that is for it.
- When the service is full, a new registration puts out the registration, **of any account**,
  that has gone longest without passing a datagram. What is in use stays; what is merely held
  goes first, whoever holds it.

## 7. What the two relays say to each other over ssh

After its greeting the server end sends its `E`. The client end, **once per session**, answers
with the service's address, the term id, the server end's voucher, the pass with every field it
signs, the term list, and the identity key's public half. Each is written in base64url, since the
words between relays are split at spaces (`src/relay/frame.h`). Old relays ignore words they do
not know. The server end checks the term list against the identity it was sent; it pins nothing.

The server end learns the user's account name and pass this way. It needs them to register, and
it is the user's own server.

## 8. Each input: absent, late, wrong, lying

- **Absent.** The service down: no COOKIE; the end's log says "no answer to hello", and it goes
  on with its other paths, asking again (6.2). No pass on the machine: the end does not try, and
  `gosh` says to run `gosh meetup-key` and sign in. The other end not registered within 60 seconds: the
  registration is dropped. The web side or a sign-in provider down: nobody can enrol or renew;
  passes already given go on working until their terms end, and terms go on rolling (the tool
  does not need the web side), so a week's outage costs only the users whose passes end that
  week. The term tool not run: no new term, and after 90 days no pass can be signed; its cron
  job's failure is the operator's to see (section 11).
- **Late.** A cookie older than ten seconds is refused; the end asks for another. An old PASS is
  refused once 1,024 behind. A pass whose term has ended gets NO. A term list older than another
  the end has seen is ignored.
- **Wrong.** Before a good cookie, nothing is answered but NO. After one: REFUSED with why, or (a
  box that does not open, a voucher that is not that user's for this service, term and pair)
  dropped. On the web: every check in section 5 that fails says, on the page, which.
- **Lying.** An end for a `P` that is not for it is passed nothing. A user can vouch for any pair
  of keys; it spends their own registrations. A provider that vouches for an address it should not
  (a tenant that sets its users' addresses itself): that is why Microsoft addresses need
  `xms_edov` or a listed tenant, and Google domains need `hd`. A pass made under terms the
  service never made: refused by the pinned identity.
- **The clock.** The service's wrong: the term tool refuses to act (section 3, step 4), and a UDP
  side with no term in span answers nothing. An end's wrong: `gosh meetup-pass` may refuse a good
  pass, or keep one whose term has ended, and the renewal warning is from the service's time, not
  the end's; the UDP side's own clock is what decides.
- **The service's own state.** Missing or unreadable at start: it does not start. Unreadable
  later: the old state stays, and it says so.

## 9. What an attacker can do, and what is not closed

| Attacker | Today | Proposed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Knows nothing of the service | Gets no answer | Gets no answer by UDP (a scanner); the web side is a web page, and gives the identity and term list to anyone who asks |
| Knows the service's identity; holds no pass | Uses the meetup for ever with one copied datagram; takes an end's place | Gets cookies, and NO with the public term list (no larger than the HELLO); makes the service do one hash a datagram, and a limited number of costly opens; sees that some `E` registers every 25 s, from where. Cannot register, pass, or take a place |
| On the path, can forge addresses; no pass | As above | Can replay only what the service did not receive. Can answer an end first with a false COOKIE, or an old NO, and so keep it from registering for a while: a denial, which dropping would give anyway; the end never records a NO, and keeps trying |
| Anyone with an account at the three providers | — | Enrols as themselves under the web side's default whitelist: **most people**. Is admitted by UDP only if the service's own hook says so; gosh's default hook says no. A blacklisted person makes a new account in a minute. Behind a narrow whitelist, as a school writes it, neither is so |
| A Microsoft tenant's admin who sets a user's address to someone else's | — | Refused: a Microsoft address is taken only with `xms_edov` or from a listed tenant |
| A former member of a school who still has a verified school address on a personal account | — | Enrols as that address under `google:.*@school\.edu` unless `hd` is also required; the design requires it |
| An address that was someone else's, given out again by the school | — | To the hook, the same person: inherits the old holder's blacklist entry and limits, and the old holder's passes stay good under that name until their terms end. (`sub` would tell them apart; left out: section 4) |
| Someone who sends a victim an enrolment link for the attacker's own key | — | Nothing: no key travels in a link; the user pastes their own. To talk a user into pasting the attacker's key, past a page that names the user's account and words that are not their own, is phishing made harder, not impossible |
| Someone who knows a user's key (their server host does) and hands them a pass of terms of their own | — | Refused once the user's machine has pinned the service's identity. **On a machine that has never enrolled, taken**: the first identity is taken on trust, and its six words printed to compare with any the service publishes elsewhere. The forged pass is then answered NO by the real service, and the user told to sign in again |
| Someone who gives a user a pass that is not for the user's key | — | `gosh meetup-pass` refuses it |
| Another user of the service | Takes an end's place, if they see the pair's name | Cannot open another's messages, register as another's end, or be passed another pair's datagrams. Has their own limits; fills the service only as far as the whole service's eviction allows |
| A server host the user logged in to | Holds the shared key: a user of the meetup for ever | For that session, is that session's server end. Learns the user's account name and pass. Afterwards holds a voucher naming an end nobody is: put out after 60 seconds. Cannot relay for itself |
| Someone who steals a user's private key | — | Is that user, until the hook refuses the key or the pass's term ends. Cannot read or join a running session |
| Someone who takes over a user's account at the provider | — | Can enrol a key of their own as that user, until the account is blacklisted |
| Someone who breaks into the web side | Sees which addresses talk and how much | Can sign passes with the current term's signing key, until an emergency roll. Holds no box key, so reads no registration. Holds no identity key, so makes no term. Can add to the blacklist, not remove from it |
| Someone who breaks into the UDP side | — | Holds the box keys of the last four terms: can read the registrations sealed to them (which account, at which address, for which peer, each `Kr`) and pose as the service by UDP until an emergency roll. Holds no signing key and no identity key. Not the content of any session |
| Someone who takes the identity key | — | Can make terms, and so passes, as the service, for anyone, until users forget the identity and enrol again under a new one. It is held by the term tool alone, never by a side that listens |
| Anyone, with huge or malformed datagrams | (the fork never opened what it was sent) | Refused by length before anything is opened; what fails to open is caught |

In every row, nobody reads or changes what the two ends say: that is sealed by the session.

**Not closed, besides what the table says:**

- **No forward secrecy for registrations** (the owner: "ok"); the remedy is an emergency roll.
- **The first identity is taken on trust**, on each machine's first enrolment.
- **The service is trusted** not to drop, and sees who talks to whom, how much, and as which
  account. It writes none of that to a log (deferred by the owner).
- **A PASSED proves nothing to the end that gets it.** The session refuses what is not its own.
- **An end's key `E` is in the clear** when it registers. mosh's own datagrams carry a counter in
  the clear.
- **A NAT that changes an end's port** costs 3.5 seconds and two round trips to mend.
- **A user's private key is a file** with no passphrase.
- **The pass and the account name reach the user's server** (section 7).
- **The regular expressions are whoever runs the service's to get right.** Full-text matching and
  lower-cased ASCII remove the commonest mistakes, not all.
- **TweetNaCl** is small and slow. The service's CPU limits are to be set from TweetNaCl measured
  on the machine it will run on. Its signature check accepts some signatures in more than one
  form; nothing here is keyed on a signature's bytes.
- **IPv4.** The UDP side listens on IPv4, as today; the answer's address field fits IPv6.
- **Cost.** Each relayed datagram is sealed once more by the sender and opened once by the
  service. This path is the last resort before ssh.

## 10. What changes in gosh, and what is new

**Done already** (groundwork): `vendor/tweetnacl/`, checked against published answers in the
check; `src/meetup/nacl.*`, the one wrapper that calls it. *It does its key agreement twice per
box, once to check for an all-zero secret and once to seal; it is to do it once, and to keep the
agreed key for a registration (section 6.1, step 4).*

**In gosh** (C++, this repository):

- `src/meetup/`: `proof.*` goes, and `answer.*` (the STUN answer) with it. New logic, each with a test of bytes and numbers and no socket:
  `pass.h` (the exact encodings of section 3, the pass's and the term list's, made and checked,
  with the shared test vectors), `terms.h` (which are good at an instant, which ended early, the
  pinned identity), `cookie.h`, `messages.h` (every kind; exact lengths), `registrations.h` (6.1,
  6.2, 6.4), `passing.h` (6.3), `admit.h` (the hook of section 4, and gosh's default, which says
  no). `serve.cc` stays the edge: the socket, the time since start, gosh's directory, loading the
  hook.
- `gosh-meetup term` (with `--now` and `--clock-ok`): the one tool that holds the identity key and
  rolls terms (section 3). Its keys and each side's are files in gosh's directory on the machine
  that runs the service, each readable by one Unix user.
- `gosh meetup-key` and `gosh meetup-pass` (with `--forget`): section 5, steps 1 and 6. Text in and
  out; gosh listens on nothing and needs no HTTPS.
- A list of 2,048 words for the six words, carried in, with its licence stated (to be chosen).
- The words between relays (section 7).
- `src/pathfinding/`: the end's half, as logic, when that part is reshaped (the rest of queue
  item 8). **In the fork today, found by the first attack:** `punch.cc` hands any probe-marked
  datagram of up to 65,528 bytes to the cipher's `decrypt`, whose 2,048-byte buffer is guarded
  only by an `assert`. Path-finding is off in gosh; this is fixed before it is switched on.
- Tests: the attacks of 2026-11-15 turned round (each thing an onlooker could do becomes a check
  that they cannot), and one for each row of section 9 that a test can reach.
- No private key, `Kr`, pass, voucher, account name or the bytes of a registration is written
  to a log.

**New, in a repository of its own, with requirements of its own:** the web side (section 5): its
SQLite database, its regular expressions and their expiry, its sign-in, its record of enrolments
(when, which account, the whole `U`, kept a year: the owner, "ok"), and its build of the UDP side
with its own hook (section 4). It shares with gosh the encodings of section 3, by their test
vectors, and the hook's declaration.

A gosh meetup and the fork's relays ignore each other; the requirements already say gosh does
not work with the fork's releases at the other end.

## 11. What whoever runs a service does

- **Once:** register the service with each sign-in provider; write the whitelist and the hook's
  lists; make three Unix users (web side, UDP side, term tool); run `gosh-meetup term` to make
  the identity and the first term; publish the identity's six words somewhere users can compare
  them; start the two sides.
- **Every day** (cron): `gosh-meetup term`. On most days it makes only the day's term list; it
  exits non-zero, and says why, if anything is wrong (the clock having gone back or jumped, for
  one). Keep the machine's clock set by NTP.
- **When someone must be put out:** add them to the blacklist, with when it ends (a year unless
  said); the hook answers the UDP side differently within seconds.
- **After a break-in of either side, or a lost key directory:** `gosh-meetup term --now`. Everyone
  pastes their key and signs in again.
- **After the identity key is taken or lost:** a new identity; every user runs
  `gosh meetup-pass --forget` and enrols again.
- **Backups:** the database and the public keys. Never a private key.

## 12. How this proposal got here

- **Revision 1**: one shared key and the clock. Attacked: twelve findings (among them that
  someone with the shared key who saw one registration kept that pair from anywhere; an
  oversized datagram would abort the meetup inside the cipher; a tag over a counter alone let a
  payload be swapped; the clock rule failed silently).
- **Revision 2** took them, with a key per user derived from a master. It kept to a shared-key
  cipher because the agent read `REQ1/SHAPE-3` as ruling out public keys; **that was a mistake:**
  it rules out a library a machine may lack, not source carried here.
- **Revision 3**: key pairs, authorities that sign users' keys by hand. Attacked: eleven findings
  (a voucher bound to nothing let a server host lock a user out for good; every stranger's
  datagram cost a costly step; answers were not tied to their requests; renewing with signatures
  would not fit; silence for every failure).
- **Revision 4** took those.
- **Revision 5**: the owner's direction: one service that signs its own passes, with term keys
  that roll, sign-in with an account the user has. Attacked: thirteen findings. Among them:
  **anyone could have enrolled as anyone**, because the design trusted the provider's address
  without naming which claim or check, and a Microsoft tenant's admin can set any address; a
  pass whose term had passed could not be refused with a reason, since the key to seal the
  refusal was gone; nobody was named to roll terms and the two sides could disagree; the
  loopback listener would take the first request from anyone; a crafted link could enrol an
  attacker's key under a victim's account; two regular-expression engines would disagree and one
  could hang the UDP side; the signing key was not kept from the UDP side; the service could be
  filled and used as a free relay.
- **Revision 6** took those; SQLite and regular expressions are the web side's, and gosh keeps
  plain text.
- **Revision 7**: the owner's answers to revision 6. The UDP side asks a hook, which a service's
  own build provides, in place of gosh's own list; a pass no good key opens is answered NO; terms
  are good by date, the date a signed one (Roughtime); enrolment is by pasting, in and out, and
  gosh listens on nothing; rates are deferred. Attacked: thirteen findings. The worst: **a term
  vouched only for itself**, so anyone who knew a user's key could make terms of their own and a
  pass under them that every check took; and Roughtime from the service itself added nothing (only
  the UDP side decides, by its own clock), while bringing a new master key, an amplifier and a
  signature per request. Also: a wrong clock on the service could end every pass, and the check
  meant to catch it compared the clock with itself; the unsealed NO answered strangers, could be
  injected, and was taken as final; the hook was unspecified, could stall the single thread, and
  linking it to the web side's database let a break-in there undo the blacklist; one term file
  held both sides' keys; variable fields undid "exact lengths"; the emergency never reached ends;
  the pasted text was unspecified and longer than macOS lets a line be; four words were 44 bits;
  days had no time zone, and "term" meant two things; and REQUIREMENTS_v15 disagreed with the
  design in five places.
- **Revision 8** (this) takes those, with the owner's answers: the identity key, pinned, signs a
  term list that ends carry and NO sends; Roughtime is dropped ("yes drop roughtime"); the clock
  is checked against what the tool last saw; NO is gated, signed by the list it carries, and never
  recorded; the hook has a contract and gosh's default says no; files per side; fixed slots; the
  pasted text and six words; UTC instants, and "term" for the keys, "span" for when their passes
  are good; list entries that expire; `sub` noted and left out; the meetup answers no STUN question. Requirements v15 is rewritten to
  match, for the owner to accept as a whole. **Not yet attacked.**

## 13. REQ1/PATH-7 stands as written

The owner asked what REQ1/PATH-7 meant ("No server on the Internet is asked anything unless a session
has fallen back to ssh, and `--stun=none` or `--punch=never` stops it then too"): a session that
works by plain UDP asks no other server anything, so that it gives nobody else its addresses. The
fourth attack found the design at odds with it only through Roughtime, which an end would have
asked outside a session. With Roughtime dropped, an end speaks to the service only while
path-finding runs, and path-finding runs only on a session that has fallen back to ssh (section
6.2); enrolling is done by the user, in a browser. So REQ1/PATH-7 needs no change, and the rewrite of
v15 leaves it alone.

## 14. The attack on revision 8 (2026-11-15): findings, to be taken in revision 9

A fifth session set to break it on paper. Verdict: "build with these changes", findings 1 to 5
before anyone builds. Kept here briefly so that they outlive the session.

1. **The first pin comes from text the web side writes.** A web break-in serves its own identity,
   term list and pass; every first enrolment pins the attacker, `--now` does not undo it, and
   "run --forget" is a ready phishing script. *Change:* the identity is pinned from the
   operator's handout before any paste (`gosh meetup-key SERVICE WORDS`), keyed by service name;
   `--forget` shows the old words and asks.
2. **The append-only file is not**: rewriting at the same length blanks entries; a restart
   reloads the doctored file; removing expired entries shortens it. *Change:* the operator's
   entries in a file the web side cannot write; the hook keeps the length and hash of what it
   last took, across restarts, and refuses a file whose prefix differs; expiry is filtered, never
   removed, but by the operator compacting; a load failure keeps the last good set and alarms.
3. **"Newer list" by made-time** lets a forward clock poison every later list, the emergency's
   included. *Change:* order lists by a counter; define what `--clock-ok` does to the kept time.
4. **The identity key is online daily for nothing**: no end checks a list's age. *Change:* sign
   only when terms change or end, and let the identity key be kept offline.
5. **REQ1/PATH-7 and REQ1/PATH-2 not squared**: `--stun=none`/`--punch=never` must stop the meetup too; and
   whether a session through the meetup has "fallen back" (REQ1/PATH-2 closes ssh once UDP works) is
   unsaid. Section 13 cites 6.2 for what 6.2 does not say.
6. **NO is under-checked**: an old authentic list replayed tells the user to sign in every
   minute, even when the list shows the pass's term good; "never record" contradicts keeping
   ended ids and ignoring older lists. *Change:* ignore a NO whose list shows the term good;
   record the highest list counter and the ended ids.
7. **The clock rule makes an outage a lockout**, and invites `--clock-ok` in cron. *Change:* after
   a forward gap do the harmless work, refuse only ending or destroying.
8. **A changed identity fails silently** (the gate never answers). *Change:* after some unanswered
   HELLOs, say "or the service's identity changed".
9. **Revocation lags** up to a KEEP (about 25 s), and a whitelist entry's removal stops only new
   enrolment. *Change:* compare the hook's counter on PASS; have the hook apply the whitelist too,
   or say so.
10. **KEEP's address** contradicts PASS's source check. *Change:* KEEP checks the source address.
11. **A listed Microsoft consumer tenant** lets personal accounts with a school address pass.
    *Change:* a domain is satisfied only by `xms_edov` or that domain's own tenant.
12. **Numbers wrong**: the term list is about 845 bytes, the pasted text about 2,000 characters;
    the version field is missing from the diagrams; eight ended-early slots overflow after two
    emergencies.
13. **No way to replace a stolen user key.** *Change:* `gosh meetup-key --new`.
14. **Smaller**: the per-`E` key cache unbounded; small-order `U` not refused; "a PASS has had
    nothing back" undefined; the renewal warning needs a registration (warn by the end's clock
    too); with rates deferred, an admitted user has an unmetered relay.
15. **Cross-references**: rates are in 6.3, the account's form in section 4; `gosh meetup-key`
    could be read as `gosh HOST`.

**Against REQUIREMENTS_v15:** REQ15/PATH-7 (finding 5); *Data about people* says a meetup has no
names, but the UDP side reads the account; the glossary's *meetup* says `gosh-server meetup`
signs, but the web side does; *words* is used for fingerprints and relay messages too; *identity*,
*term list* and *span* are missing, and *term*'s 360 days is ambiguous; `gosh-meetup` is not in
REQ15/NAME-1 or REQ15/CMD-2; *Scope decisions* cites the proposal's section 7 for section 9;
"signs in once a year" is 270 to 360 days; the inputs table's meetup row misses a lying address
and time; REQ15/PATH-5's "just shown" versus 25 s of lag; REQ15/PATH-4 cannot be tested with a hook
that says no (a test hook is needed); sign-in providers are not among the people it touches.

**Not decided by the attacker:** whether the largest PASS fits the cipher's 2,048-byte buffer;
Apple's `email_verified` as a string; `xms_edov` for personal accounts; whether one machine may
hold passes for several services.
